\section{Conclusion}
\label{chap:conclusion}
In this thesis, our concept Hover has been invented, described, and tested. Based on the topics presented, the conclusion to the project and the research questions are:

\textbf{RQ1} asked: “\textit{Which \textbf{background knowledge} is needed for developing an alternative game concept?}”. Part \ref{part:prestudy} answered this research question by defining alternative games and describing relevant game design principles from studying how to design enjoyable games, reward systems, and player types. Six alternative game concepts were invented, which led to our decision to develop Hover.

\textbf{RQ2} studied \textit{how game design elements and social interaction affect the players’ \textbf{engagement} in our game concept}. The results show that existing relationships between players affected their willingness to engage in the game. Out of the social game design element, the friend tracking functionality seems to engage the players the most, while reactions and challenges the least. The social screens, such as the feed and the leaderboard, are where the participants engaged the most; both screens share the activity and progress of other players. Notifications also positively affect the players’ engagement as they reminded them to hover. Lastly, the results reveal that rewards such as points and rewards did not, to a large degree, affect the player’s engagement. 

\textbf{RQ3} looked at \textit{how game design elements and social interaction affect the players’ \textbf{motivation} in our game concept}. The conclusion is that points and social factors significantly impacted the players’ motivation. The results show that players were motivated to use the friend tracking functionality as double points gave them an incentive for hovering. The social pressure among friends and the position on the leaderboard affected the players’ motivation to hover. Points affected the motivation to a large degree; however, suggestions indicate that using points as a game currency would enhance motivation. 

In \textbf{RQ4}, the focus was \textit{how game design elements and social interaction affect the players’ \textbf{enjoyment} in our game concept}. The conclusion is that some game design elements and the social interaction between players affected their enjoyment positively. One clear example is the Hover with Friends functionality that lets players collaborate to get more points. Overall, most participants enjoyed Hover; however, some game design elements, such as reactions and challenges, did not clearly affect participants’ enjoyment, with most participants being neutral to these elements. 

Finally, \textbf{RQ5} looked at \textit{how the use of our game concept affects the players’ \textbf{habits}}. Based on the results, Hover did not change the participants’ habits. The most prominent reasons for this were the duration of testing and the ongoing pandemic. 

The overall conclusion is that social game design elements in Hover affect players the most. Unfortunately, the testing and conclusion are to a large degree influenced by the short testing period and the Covid-19 pandemic. Consequently, we suggest conducting a new experiment under normal circumstances, with the propositions for further work given in Chapter \ref{chap:further_work}.
