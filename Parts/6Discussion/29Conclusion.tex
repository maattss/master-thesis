\section{Conclusion}
\label{chap:conclusion}
In this thesis, our concept Hover has been invented, described, and tested. Based on the topics presented, the conclusion to the project and the research questions are:

\textbf{RQ1} asked: \textit{“Which \textbf{background knowledge} is needed for developing an alternative game concept?”}. Part \ref{part:prestudy} answered this research question by describing relevant game design principles and defining alternative games. Six alternative game concepts were invented, which led to our decision to develop Hover.

\textbf{RQ2} studied \textit{how game design elements and social interaction affect the players’ \textbf{engagement} in our game concept}. The findings suggest that points, rewards, reactions, and challenges do not affect the player’s engagement. However, the results show that existing relationships between players affected their willingness to engage in the game and with other players. Notifications also positively affect engagement.

\textbf{RQ3} looked at \textit{how game design elements and social interaction affect the players’ \textbf{motivation} in our game concept}. The conclusion is that points and social factors (including game design elements such as leaderboard and social double points) significantly impacted the players’ motivation.

In \textbf{RQ4}, the focus was \textit{how game design elements and social interaction affect the players’ \textbf{enjoyment} in our game concept}. The conclusion is that some game design elements and the social interaction between players affected their enjoyment positively. One clear example is the Hover with Friends functionality.

Finally, \textbf{RQ5}, looked at \textit{how the use of our game concept affects the players’ habits}. Based on the results, Hover did not change the participants’ habits. 

The overall conclusion is that social game design elements in Hover affect players the most. Unfortunately, the testing and conclusion are highly influenced by the short testing period and the Covid-19 pandemic. Consequently, we suggest conducting a new experiment under normal circumstances, with the propositions given in Chapter \ref{chap:further_work} - Further Work included.
