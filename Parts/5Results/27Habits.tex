\section{Results on Change in Habits}
\label{chap:habits}
In this chapter, results related to \textit{RQ5: How does the use of our game concept affect the players’ habits?} will be presented. As in the previous three chapters, data from the chapter will be used to discuss this research question in Chapter \ref{chap:discussion}.

It can be difficult to find reliable and objective data that reveals changed habits in a project of this size because it requires observations to be made over an extended period. Measuring the different activity levels between the two weeks is insufficient. Thus, we depend on the players’ perceived change in habits. The data described below are collected from the questionnaire and conducted interviews.

\subsection{Results on Change in Habits from Questionnaire}
The questionnaire included eight statements and two questions to investigate changes in players’ habits. The statements, presented in Table \ref{tab:habits_statements}, are grouped by their respective categories (culture, education, exercise, and social). For each of these categories, the participants had to state whether they felt their habits were changed and if the game made them want to visit such locations more often. Like the previous chapters, a three-point Likert scale is used for improving readability.

As explained earlier in the chapter, the table uses a three-point Likert scale for improved readability. Further investigation confirms that this is the case in S28.

The players were given two closed (yes/no) questions at the end of the questionnaire (see Figure \ref{fig:habits_questions}). Q22 examines if the player wants to continue using a game like Hover. The results from this question were split between the two groups. The student society leaned towards \textit{"no"}, while the group of classmates was more on the \textit{"yes"} side. In total, a slight majority of 53\% stated that they would not like to continue using a similar game concept.

Q23 asked the participants if they thought they would have used the game more if it had not been an ongoing pandemic. Similar to the previous question, the results are not united between the groups. 86\% in the group of classmates responded \textit{"yes"} to the question, while only 50\% in the student society group did the same. In total, more than half of the respondents (63\%) answered \textit{"yes"}.

For the participants responding \textit{"yes"}, a follow-up question of "Why?" was asked. Common for most of the responses was that they would have visited more places, such as concert venues, restaurants, and training centers. 

\textit{“Normally I would probably go to campus almost every day, but during the test period, I did not go at all.” \footnotemark[\value{footnote}]}

\textit{“In a normal situation, there would be more activities to do. A lot is closed, and people are more at home than usual.” \footnotemark[\value{footnote}]}

One participant reported that they avoided campus due to infection control and felt there was a higher threshold for going to cultural events:

\textit{“I worked at home the first week of the test period because I wanted to avoid campus due to infection control. Also, there is a higher threshold for going to any type of cultural happenings.” \footnotemark[\value{footnote}]}

Similar to the other topics in the questionnaire, the participants could also leave general comments regarding their change in habits. One participant stated that it was difficult to change habits because they did not use the game continuously. Another reported that they thought their habits could change if the testing would have lasted longer. 

\textit{“Since I did not use Hover continuously during the test period, it was difficult to change habits.” \footnotemark[\value{footnote}]}

\textit{“My habits could probably change more if I used the app for a longer period than two weeks.” \footnotemark[\value{footnote}]}

Two of the participants commented that though Hover did not change their habits, they felt that it induced more value to the activities.

\textit{“It did not change my habits directly. More so that it gave schoolwork an extra added value.” \footnotemark[\value{footnote}]}

\textit{“I do not think Hover got me to visit the Student Society/campus/training centers more frequently, but it was a nice addition that made these activities more rewarding.” \footnotemark[\value{footnote}]}

\footnotetext{Translated from Norwegian}

\subsection{Results on Change in Habits from Interviews}
In the interviews, we tried to investigate to what degree the participant’s habits were affected by the pandemic. All of the interviewees stated that they, to a large degree, were affected by the national restrictions and what they felt comfortable doing. One participant said that their activity level was probably around 20\% of their normal non-pandemic behavior. 

\subsection{Summary}
This chapter described results related to change in habits (RQ5) from the questionnaire and interviews. The results also presented differences between the two groups and a selection of statements made by the participants regarding their perceived change in habits. The chapter is the last one in Part \ref{part:results}, concluding the presentation of data gathered during the test period.
