\section{Change in Habits Data}
\label{chap:habits}
In this chapter, results related to \textit{RQ5: How does the use of our game concept affect the players’ habits?} will be presented. As in the previous two chapters, data from the chapter will be used to discuss this research question in Chapter \ref{chap:discussion}.

It can be difficult to find reliable and objective data that reveals changed habits in a project of this size because it requires observations to be made over an extended period . Measuring the different activity levels between the two weeks is insufficient. Thus, we depend on the players’ perceived change in habits. The data described below are collected from the questionnaire and conducted interviews.

\subsection{Questionnaire}
The questionnaire included eight statements and two questions to investigate the players’ habits. The statements, presented in Table \ref{tab:habits_statements}, are grouped by their respective categories (culture, education, exercise, and social). For each of these categories, the participants had to state whether they felt their habits were changed and if the game made them want to visit such locations more often. Like the previous chapters, a three-point Likert scale is used for readability. Additionally, the average responses for the two groups are calculated (Avg S and Avg C).

As seen in the table, the results were very one-sided, and the majority of the participants disagreed with all eight statements. However,there was a clear difference between the two groups. The student society group’s averages (Avg S) were below  1.43 for all eight statements, which roughly equals \textit{strongly disagree}. The classmates’ average answers (Avg C) were between 1.71 and 3.43, with disagree and agree as the most common answers. In S30 and S31 about educational habits, the difference between the two groups is particularly prominent.

The players were given two closed (yes/no) questions at the end of the questionnaire. Q22 examines if the player wants to continue using a game like Hover (see \ref{fig:habits_continue}). The results from this question were split between the two groups. The student society leaned towards \textit{“no”}, while the group of classmates was more on the textit{“yes”} side. In total, a slight majority of 52.6\% stated that they would not like to continue using a similar game concept.

Q23 asked the participants if they think they had used the game more if it had not been an ongoing pandemic. Similar to the previous question, the results are not united between the groups. 85.7\% in the group of classmates responded \textit{“yes”} to the question, while only 50\% in the student society group did the same. Thus, these results imply that the use of Hover among the group of classmates was more affected by the pandemic than the student society group. In total, more than half of the respondents (63.2\%) answered \textit{“yes”}.

For the participants responding \textit{“yes”}, a follow-up question of “Why?” was asked. Common for most of the responses was that they would have visited more places, such as concert venues, restaurants, and training centers. 

\textit{“Normally I would probably go to campus almost every day, but during the test period, I did not go at all.” \footnotemark[\value{footnote}]}

\textit{“In a normal situation, there would be more activities to do. A lot is closed, and people are more at home than usual.” \footnotemark[\value{footnote}]}

One participant reported that they avoided campus due to infection control and felt there was a higher threshold for going to cultural events.

\textit{“I worked at home the first week of the test period because I wanted to avoid campus due to infection control. Also, there is a higher threshold for going to any type of cultural happenings.” \footnotemark[\value{footnote}]}

Similar to the other topics in the questionnaire, the participants could also leave general comments regarding players’ habits. One participant stated that it was difficult to change habits because they did not use the game continuously. Another reported that they thought their habits could change if the testing would have lasted longer. 

\textit{“Since I did not use Hover continuously during the test period, it was difficult to change habits.” \footnotemark[\value{footnote}]}

\textit{“My habits could probably change more if I used the app for a longer period than two weeks.” \footnotemark[\value{footnote}]}

Two of the participants commented that though Hover did not change their habits, they felt that it induced more value to the activities.

\textit{“It did not change my habits directly. More so that it gave schoolwork an extra added value.” \footnotemark[\value{footnote}]}

\textit{“I do not think Hover got me to visit the Student Society/campus/training centers more frequently, but it was a nice addition that made these activities more rewarding.” \footnotemark[\value{footnote}]}

\subsection{Interviews}
In the interviews, we tried to investigate to what degree the participant’s habits were affected by the pandemic. All of the interviewees stated that they, to a large degree, were affected by the national restrictions and what they felt comfortable doing. One participant said that their activity level was probably around 20\% of their normal non-pandemic behavior. Thus, based on the interviews, there seems to be a strong relationship between the users’ activity level and the national recommendations caused by the pandemic.
