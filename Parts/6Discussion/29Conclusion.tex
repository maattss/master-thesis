\section{Conclusion}
\label{chap:conclusion}

In the specialization project, we studied alternative games and contributed to the research field with the alternative game framework. Moreover, in this thesis project,  we have developed and tested our game concept Hover. To fulfill our research goal, we answer our research questions:

\textbf{RQ1} asked: \textit{“Which background knowledge is needed for developing an alternative game concept?”}. Part \ref{part:prestudy} answered this research question by describing relevant game design principles and defining alternative games. Six alternative game concepts were invented, which resulted in Hover.

\textbf{RQ2} studied \textit{how game design elements and social interaction affect the players’ engagement in our game concept}. The conclusion is that points, rewards, reactions, and challenges do not affect the player’s engagement in Hover; however, the results suggest that existing relationships between players affected their willingness to engage in the game and with other players. Moreover, notifications positively affect engagement.

\textbf{RQ3} looked at \textit{how game design elements and social interaction affect the players’ motivation in our game concept}. The conclusion is that points and social factors (including game design elements such as leaderboard and social double points) significantly impacted the players’ motivation.

In \textbf{RQ4}, the focus was \textit{how game design elements and social interaction affect the players’ enjoyment in our game concept}. The conclusion is that some game design elements and the social interaction between players affected their enjoyment positively. One clear example is the Hover with Friends functionality.

Finally, \textbf{RQ5}, looked at \textit{how the use of our game concept affects the players’ habits}. Based on the results, Hover did not change the participants’ habits in our game concept. 

The overall conclusion is that social game design elements affect players the most in our concept. Unfortunately, the circumstances surrounding the Covid-19 pandemic impact the validity of the results. Consequently, we suggest conducting a new experiment under normal circumstances, including the propositions given in Chapter \ref{chap:further_work} - Further Work.
